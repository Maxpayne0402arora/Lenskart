Vision Check for Kids — Success Metrics

Lenskart Product Metrics Framework • Co-founder Review Edition

NO-EMOJI EXECUTIVE FORMAT
Block 1 — Primary Objective

North Star Metric

Attended under-18 eye tests attributable to Vision Check that resulted in a prescription change, per month.
Attended, not booked — an unattended booking is worth zero. And resulted in a change, because a referral that finds nothing means the signal fired wrongly. Deliberately not app sessions or store walk-ins: both rise when the product gets pushier, not when a child sees better.

Block 3 — Long-Term Business Impact

Outcomes

Prescription freshness
Share of under-18 prescriptions on file under 12 months old. The number this product exists to move, measurable from existing order data before a line ships.
Time to correction
Median days from session to glasses in the child's hands. Not to booking, not to test.
Monitoring loop retention
Share of MONITOR results where the child plays again at the scheduled recheck. If low, the monitor branch is a polite way of dropping people.
Tier-2+ share of sessions
If this only reaches metro parents on good phones, it isn't serving the market Lenskart is growing into. Screen calibration is the likely culprit.
Block 4 — Diagnostic Reliability

Signal Quality

Accuracy is the wrong metric. At ~15% prevalence, classifying every child CLEAR scores 85% accuracy and catches nobody. Precision, recall and NPV are tracked separately because they cost different amounts to obtain.
Precision (PPV) — free
TP / (TP + FP)
Of children referred, the share who genuinely needed correction. Every referred child walks into a store, so we get ground truth on all of them.
Recall — has to be bought
TP / (TP + FN)
Cleared children never come in, so this is invisible by default. Each month, randomly sample a cohort of CLEAR children and offer a free in-store test regardless of result. Budgeted, not aspirational.
NPV — the safety number
TN / (TN + FN)
The P0 guardrail in block 5 is 1 − NPV, so that trigger is the bar. No separate number, deliberately.
Ground truth box
"Needed correction" = the in-store refraction issued a new prescription or changed an existing one by ≥ 0.50 D in either eye. Fixed before launch so precision can't be improved by redefining a hit.
Test-retest box
Same child, two sessions seven days apart, no clinical change expected. The spread between readings is what defines a meaningful change, so it must be measured before the MONITOR and REFER thresholds are set. Guessing a threshold and hoping reliability supports it is the failure mode.
Block 5 — Risk Control & Non-Negotiables

Guardrails & Anti-Metric

Guardrail Trigger Action
False negatives (P0) — CLEAR children needing power change within 90 days > 10% of CLEAR Pull the feature
False positives — REFER results where store test finds no change needed > 40% of REFER Tighten the threshold. Guardrail on commercial drift — loosening REFER turns this into a footfall driver
Child-facing leakage — clinical language reaching a child Any instance P0 defect, not a metric
Consent withdrawal > 5% Consent screen over-reaching, or delivery unwelcome
Reminder opt-out > 20% Cadence is wrong
Anti-metric — watch, never optimise

Frame and lens attach rate on the resulting visit.

It will rise, and that's fine. It must not appear in anyone's goals, on a decision dashboard, or in any experiment's success criteria. Optimising it converts a health check into a sales trigger and parents feel it within one cycle.

Coupled alarm: if attach rate grows faster than attendance for two consecutive months, audit the referral threshold and the result copy.
